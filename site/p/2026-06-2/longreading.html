A

A) Five hundred years ago, forests covered much of the Iberian peninsula. But that soon changed. Centuries of wars and invasions, agricultural expansion and woodcutting for firewood and shipping wiped out most of the woods and transformed places like Matamorisca, a small village in northern Spain, into degraded landscapes.

五百年前，森林覆盖了伊比利亚半岛的大部分地区。但这种情况很快发生了改变。数百年的战争和入侵、农业扩张，以及为取暖和造船而砍伐木材，使大部分森林消失，并将西班牙北部的小村庄Matamorisca等地变成了退化的景观。

B

B) The region's dry climate and depleted soils would be a recipe for disaster in your average program to replant trees, but for the Amsterdam-based Land Life Company it's an ideal place. "We typically operate where nature does not come back by itself," says Jurrian Ruys, its CEO. "We go where there are rougher conditions in terms of weather, with rough or very hot summers." In Matamorisca, they intervened in 17 barren hectares owned by the regional government and peppered them with their signature device: a biodegradable(可生物降解的) cardboard container they call the cocoon which can hold 25 litres of water underground to aid a seedling's first year. Around 16,000 trees, including oaks, ashes, walnuts, rowans, and whitebeams, were planted in May 2018, and the company reports that 96% of them survived that year's burning hot summer without extra irrigation, a critical milestone for a young tree.

该地区干燥的气候和贫瘠的土壤，对于一般的植树造林计划来说将是灾难的根源，但对于总部位于阿姆斯特丹的Land Life Company而言，这里却是理想之地。该公司的首席执行官Jurrian Ruys说：“我们通常在自然无法自行恢复的地方开展工作。我们会前往天气条件更为恶劣、夏季较为艰苦或极其炎热的地方。”在Matamorisca，他们对地方政府拥有的17公顷贫瘠土地进行了干预，并在其中布置了他们的标志性设备：一种可生物降解的纸板容器，他们称之为“茧”，可以在地下储存25升水，以帮助幼苗度过第一年。2018年5月，他们种下了约1.6万棵树，包括橡树、白蜡树、核桃树、花楸树和白檀树。公司报告称，其中96%的树木在当年酷热的夏季中没有额外灌溉也存活了下来，这对幼树而言是一个关键的里程碑。

C

C) "Does nature return by itself?" asks Arnout Asjes, Land Life Company's chief technology officer, who oversees a mix of drone(无人机) and satellite imagery, big data analysis, soil enhancement, QR(二维码) tagging and site-specific tree configuration designs. "Probably, but it can take decades or hundreds of years, so we are speeding things up."

Land Life Company的首席技术官Arnout Asjes负责统筹无人机和卫星影像、大数据分析、土壤改良、二维码标记以及因地制宜的树木配置设计。他问道：“自然会自行恢复吗？或许会，但可能需要几十年甚至几百年，所以我们正在加快这一进程。”

D

D) His company belongs to a global movement of organisations trying to save degraded or deforested areas, ranging from fertile tropical lowlands to dry hills in temperate(温带的) regions. Spurred by global biodiversity loss and climate change, these groups are pushing the boundary of how to revive forest cover. "It's not a theoretical proposition," says Walter Vergara, a forest and climate specialist at the World Resources Institute(WRI). "It requires the right incentives, the right stakeholders, the right analysis and sufficient capital, but it can happen."

他的公司属于一个全球性运动，该运动由一系列试图拯救退化或遭到砍伐的地区的组织组成，这些地区从肥沃的热带低地到温带地区的干燥丘陵不等。在全球生物多样性丧失和气候变化的推动下，这些组织正在拓展恢复森林覆盖的可能性。世界资源研究所（WRI）的森林与气候专家Walter Vergara说：“这不是一个理论上的设想。它需要正确的激励机制、正确的利益相关者、正确的分析和充足的资金，但这是可以实现的。”

E

E) How these factors come together for a particular project—and whether saving destroyed woods is even possible—depends on what kind of ecosystem you have in mind. Secondary forests in the Amazon are different from Texas pines recovering after wildfires or the Arctic timber woods that cover much of Sweden. Each provides different reasons for reforestation programs and each has particular needs. In the dry conditions around Matamorisca and similar areas in Spain, Land Life Company worries about rapid desertification(沙漠化). As its focus is on restoring an ecosystem, it works with organisations that don't expect their money back.

这些因素如何结合到某个具体项目中，以及拯救遭到破坏的森林是否真的可行，取决于你设想的生态系统类型。亚马孙地区的次生林，与野火后正在恢复的得克萨斯州松林，或覆盖瑞典大部分地区的北极森林，都各不相同。每种生态系统都有不同的理由促使人们实施重新造林计划，也各自有特别的需求。在Matamorisca周边及西班牙类似地区的干燥环境中，Land Life Company担心的是迅速发生的沙漠化。由于该公司的重点是恢复生态系统，因此它与那些不指望收回资金的组织合作。

F

F) With around 600 hectares replanted around the world since 2015 and another 1,100 planned for this year, the company's drive fits into the Bonn Challenge, a global effort to restore 150 million hectares of the world's deforested and degraded land by 2020. That's an area roughly the size of Iran or Mongolia. By 2030, the goal is to reach 350 million hectares—20% more land than India. These targets include both the rehabilitation of forested areas that have lost density or look a bit weak (a process known as 'restoration' in forestry jargon) and the recovery of forest cover in areas where it was completely cleared (what's known as 'reforestation').

自2015年以来，该公司已在世界各地重新种植了约600公顷土地，今年还计划再种植1100公顷，这项事业与“波恩挑战”相契合。“波恩挑战”是一项全球性行动，旨在到2020年恢复世界上1.5亿公顷被砍伐和退化的土地。这一面积大致相当于伊朗或蒙古的国土面积。到2030年，目标是达到3.5亿公顷，比印度的国土面积多20%。这些目标既包括对森林密度下降或状况有所衰退的林区进行修复（林业术语称为“恢复”），也包括在森林覆盖曾被完全清除的地区恢复森林覆盖（即所谓的“重新造林”）。

G

G) That global target is broken into smaller pieces and takes shape in Latin America as the 20x20 Initiative, an effort to contribute 20 million hectares to the overall goal by initiating small to medium projects with the political backing of governments. Unlike Land Life Company, this region-wide project makes the economic and business case for reforestation, even though they are after biodiversity conservation. "You need to bring in private sector money," says WRI's Vergara, who leads the initiative, "and that capital needs to see a return on their investment." A study he led predicts Latin America would see an estimated net present value of about $23bn over a 50-year period if it reaches its goal.

这一全球目标被分解成更小的部分，在拉丁美洲则以“20x20倡议”的形式展开。这项倡议在各国政府的政治支持下，通过启动中小型项目，为总体目标贡献2000万公顷土地。与Land Life Company不同，这一覆盖整个地区的项目着眼于重新造林的经济和商业价值，尽管其目的在于保护生物多样性。负责领导该倡议的世界资源研究所Vergara说：“你需要引入私营部门的资金，而这些资金需要看到投资回报。”他领导的一项研究预测，如果拉丁美洲实现这一目标，那么在50年间预计可获得约230亿美元的净现值。

H

H) Money can come from timber sales in sustainably managed forests or from harvesting 'non-wood products' like nuts, oils and fruits from the trees. You can take note of how much carbon dioxide your forest is capturing and sell carbon credits to companies keen to offset their emissions. Or you can even grow the forest hoping that biodiversity will attract ecotourists who'd pay for lodging, birdwatching tours and meals.

资金可以来自可持续经营森林的木材销售，也可以来自采集树上的坚果、油料和水果等“非木材产品”。你可以记录森林吸收了多少二氧化碳，并向那些急于抵消自身排放的公司出售碳信用额度。或者，你甚至可以培育森林，希望生物多样性能够吸引生态旅游者，让他们为住宿、观鸟之旅和餐饮付费。

I

I) Still, these backers are not your high-street bank. Money for the 20x20 Initiative comes mostly from financial organisations with triple goal—a modest return on their investment, environmental benefits and social gains—known as impact investors. Take the German fund 12Tree for instance, one of the 20x20 partners. It put $9.5 million into Cuango, a 1,455-hectare property on the Caribbean coast of Panama that combines a commercial cocoa tree plantation with timber extraction from a sustainably managed secondary forest. With their money, they reforested a former cattle ranch, provided high-quality jobs for the surrounding communities and made a return on their investment.

不过，这些支持者并不是普通的商业银行。“20x20倡议”的资金主要来自具有三重目标的金融机构：获得适度的投资回报、取得环境效益以及实现社会收益，这些机构被称为影响力投资者。以德国基金12Tree为例，它是“20x20倡议”的合作伙伴之一。该基金向Cuango投入了950万美元。Cuango位于巴拿马加勒比海沿岸，占地1455公顷，将商业可可种植园与从可持续经营的次生林中采伐木材结合起来。借助这笔资金，他们在一处曾经的牧牛场上重新造林，为周边社区提供了高质量的就业机会，并获得了投资回报。

J

J) Even on land cleared decades ago and currently used by farmers, some crops can coexist with the forest, if you find the right balance. Although not technically reforestation, agroforestry (a system of land use in which harvestable trees or shrubs are grown among or around crops or on pasture land, as a means of preserving or enhancing the productivity of the land) provides an opportunity for small-holder farmers to sustain their livelihoods while adding some forest cover to their farms.

即使是在数十年前被清理、目前由农民使用的土地上，只要找到适当的平衡，一些农作物也可以与森林共存。农林业虽然在技术上不属于重新造林，但它是一种土地利用系统，即在农作物之间或周围、或者牧场上种植可采收的树木或灌木，以保持或提高土地的生产力。它为小农户维持生计提供了机会，同时也能在农场中增加一定的森林覆盖。

K

K) A global project called Breedcafs is studying how trees behave in coffee farms, hoping to find crop varieties that manage to grow under the shade of trees. Coffee grows naturally in such forests, so copying that in farms is taking the crop back to its roots. "By reintroducing trees in the landscape, we impact positively humidity, rain capture, soil conservation and biodiversity preservation," says coffee expert Benoit Bertrand, who leads the project from the French Agricultural Research Centre for International Development. Bertrand is analysing which of dozens of coffee varieties fits better for this system. A similar approach can be applied to land with cocoa, vanilla and fruit trees.

一项名为Breedcafs的全球性项目正在研究树木在咖啡农场中的生长状况，希望找到能够在树荫下生长的作物品种。咖啡在这类森林中自然生长，因此在农场中复制这种环境，就是让这种作物回归其根源。该项目由法国国际发展农业研究中心的咖啡专家Benoit Bertrand负责。他说：“通过在景观中重新引入树木，我们能够积极改善湿度、雨水截留、土壤保护和生物多样性保护。”Bertrand正在分析数十种咖啡品种，以确定哪一种更适合这一系统。类似的方法也可以应用于种植可可、香草和果树的土地。

L

L) Not every piece of land is fit for reforestation. Vergara's partners look for safe investments, and even Land Life Company only runs major projects in what they consider "low-risk" countries. "We tend to avoid large-scale operations in countries in some parts of the Middle East or Africa where the permanence is not secured," says Ruys.

并非每一块土地都适合重新造林。Vergara的合作伙伴寻找的是安全的投资，甚至Land Life Company也只在他们认为“低风险”的国家开展大型项目。Ruys说：“在中东或非洲的一些国家，由于无法确保森林的长期存续，我们往往会避免在那里开展大规模项目。”

M

M) But in the right location, perhaps all you need is time. In the Central Pacific of Costa Rica, the 330-hectare National Wildlife Refuge Baru looks nothing like the cattle ranch it was before 1987, when Jack Ewing decided to turn this large estate into an ecotourism destination. Instead of intervening, a friend told him to let nature have its way. Baru's former grazing lands are now thriving woods and the property boasts over 150 hectares of secondary forests with no intervention from humans. In the past 10 years, animals such as howling monkeys, scarlet macaws and even migratory cougars have returned to the refuge's land, boosting tourism and revitalising the ecosystem. Ewing, now 75, explains this success using the words his friend chose three decades ago: "In Costa Rica, when you stop managing the shrubs, the jungle comes back for its revenge."

但在合适的地点，也许你所需要的只是一点时间。在哥斯达黎加中太平洋地区，占地330公顷的Baru国家野生动物保护区与1987年以前的牧牛场完全不同。那一年，Jack Ewing决定将这片大型地产改造成生态旅游目的地。一位朋友告诉他，与其进行干预，不如听任自然发展。如今，Baru昔日的牧场已经变成了繁茂的森林，这片地产拥有超过150公顷的次生林，且没有受到人类干预。在过去10年里，吼猴、猩红金刚鹦鹉，甚至迁徙的美洲狮等动物重新回到了保护区的土地上，推动了旅游业发展，也使生态系统恢复了活力。现年75岁的Ewing用那位朋友30年前说过的话解释了这一成功：“在哥斯达黎加，当你不再管理灌木丛时，丛林就会回来复仇。”
